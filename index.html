The Four Quadrants

Every KPI belongs to one of four domains. Like a doctor taking vital signs, you need all four quadrants to diagnose where the economy truly is. Each card has a sparkline for instant trend context, and a live FRED chart you can load inline.

💳
Credit & Debt
Dalio's Engine Room
Yield Curve (2s10s Spread) Watching
+0.30%
The difference between 10-year and 2-year Treasury yields. Normally positive. When inverted (negative), a recession typically follows 12–18 months later. Re-steepening after a long inversion is often when the recession actually arrives.
🎓 If borrowing for 10 years is cheaper than for 2 years, markets expect rates to fall — which usually means economic trouble is coming.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 FRED: T10Y2Y
Re-steepening after inversion — late cycle warning
High Yield Credit Spreads (HY OAS) Elevated
320 bps
Extra interest companies pay to borrow vs. the government. Above 500–600 bps signals serious distress. Watch for spikes — they arrive fast and predict equity drawdowns with a short lag.
🎓 A bank charging a risky friend 8% while charging a reliable friend 2%. The 6% gap is the "spread." When that gap explodes, the whole lending market freezes up.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 FRED: BAMLH0A0HYM2
Watch for spike above 500bps as recession signal
M2 Money Supply (YoY %) Recovering
+3.2%
All money in circulation plus savings accounts, year-over-year. When it contracts (as in 2022–23, first time since the Depression), deflationary risk rises dramatically. Back positive now — deflationary shock receding.
🎓 Think of M2 as the total tokens in a board game. More tokens = prices go up. Fewer tokens = the game slows. In 2022 they actually removed tokens for the first time since the 1930s.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 FRED: M2SL
Back positive YoY — deflationary shock receding
Real Fed Funds Rate Restrictive
~2.1%
Fed Funds Rate minus PCE inflation. When positive and above nominal GDP growth, the Fed is actively slowing the economy. Historically, rates this restrictive have always eventually caused a recession or financial event.
🎓 If you borrow at 5% but inflation is 3%, your real cost is 2%. If inflation falls to 1%, your real cost jumps to 4% — much more painful. A high real rate is like putting the economy's car in second gear.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 FRED: FEDFUNDS minus PCE
~2%+ real rate — historically contractionary

Inflation & Prices
The Temperature of the Machine
5yr/5yr Forward Inflation Expectations Above Target
2.41%
What the market expects inflation to average in years 5–10 from now. Strips out short-term noise. The Fed watches this obsessively. Above 2.5% = inflation expectations getting unanchored = very bad for bonds.
🎓 Not "what will the weather be tomorrow?" but "what do you expect average summers to feel like in 2035?" It tells you the long-term mood, not just today's weather.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 FRED: T5YIFR
~2.4% — marginally elevated vs 2% target
CPI Inflation (YoY %) Declining
~2.9%
Consumer Price Index — the most widely-cited inflation measure. Tracks the price of a basket of everyday goods. Fed target is 2%. Above 4% = the Fed will tighten hard. Below 2% = risk of deflation.
🎓 If your grocery bill was €100 last year and is €103 today, that's 3% CPI. The Fed's job is to keep that number near 2% — not too hot, not too cold, like Goldilocks' porridge.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 FRED: CPIAUCSL
Trending down but sticky — last mile problem
CAPE Ratio (Shiller P/E) Stretched
~35×
Stock market price divided by 10 years of inflation-adjusted earnings. Long-run average is ~16×. Above 30× = expensive. Above 35× implies historically poor returns for the next decade. Not a timing tool — a return expectation tool.
🎓 If you buy a shop for €350,000 that earns €10,000/year, you'd wait 35 years to get your money back. CAPE above 35 says investors are paying 35 years of earnings upfront. That's risky.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 FRED: CAPE · multpl.com
Top decile. Poor 10yr forward returns implied.
PPI vs. CPI Spread Compressing
Narrow
PPI = what companies pay for inputs. CPI = what consumers pay in stores. When PPI > CPI, costs rise faster than companies can charge customers — profit margins get squeezed. Bad for equities.
🎓 A pizza shop pays more for flour and cheese (PPI up) but can't raise pizza prices fast enough (CPI lower). The shop earns less. Multiply that by every company in the economy — that's an earnings recession.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 FRED: PPIACO
Spread narrowing — margin pressure easing

Labor & Growth
The Heartbeat of the Economy
Initial Jobless Claims (Weekly) Stable
218k
How many people filed for unemployment benefits last week. Released every Thursday morning. The earliest tremor of labor market weakness — shows up here months before official unemployment rises above thresholds.
🎓 Think of it as the number of people who just got fired this week. If that number suddenly jumps from 200k to 350k over a few months, something is breaking in the economy — and this is the very first place it shows.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 FRED: ICSA (every Thursday)
~218k/week — still healthy but watch the trend
Sahm Rule Recession Indicator Below Trigger
0.20
Triggers when the 3-month average unemployment rate rises 0.5 percentage points above its 12-month low. 100% accurate recession predictor since 1970. Zero false signals. When it fires, act immediately.
🎓 Like a smoke detector tuned to ignore small cooking smoke but instantly alarm at real fire. The Sahm Rule ignores normal unemployment fluctuations but triggers the moment a real systemic deterioration begins.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 FRED: SAHMREALTIME
Any reading ≥ 0.50 = recession signal. Act.
JOLTS Quits Rate Declining
2.1%
Percentage of workers who voluntarily quit. High = workers confident they'll find something better. Declining = workers scared to quit = labor market softening. Leads official unemployment by months.
🎓 If your friend leaves a decent job because they know they'll find something better tomorrow — the market is hot. If they stay in a job they hate out of fear — the market is cooling. This measures how brave workers feel collectively.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 FRED: JTSQUR (monthly, JOLTS)
Declining from peak — labor confidence softening
US Dollar Index (DXY) Strong
107.2
Value of the USD vs. a basket of world currencies. A strong dollar tightens global financial conditions, hurts EM economies with dollar-denominated debt, and compresses US multinational earnings. Dollar peak often coincides with market inflection points.
🎓 If you borrowed in dollars and the dollar strengthens vs. your local currency, your debt just got bigger in local terms — even though you borrowed the same amount. A strong dollar is a hidden tax on the global economy.

📊 FRED: DTWEXBGS

Market Psychology
Howard Marks's Domain
Equity Risk Premium (ERP) Near Zero
~0.3%
Extra return you get for owning stocks vs. risk-free bonds. Formula: (1 ÷ CAPE) − 10yr Treasury yield. Near zero means bonds are almost as attractive as stocks — historically precedes poor equity returns or sharp corrections.
🎓 If the government pays 5% risk-free, but stocks might only return 4% (with all that volatility and risk), why own stocks? When the bonus for taking risk disappears, smart money moves to bonds — and stocks must fall to restore the premium.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 Calculate: (1/CAPE) − DGS10
Near zero — stocks expensive relative to bonds
AAII Sentiment (Bull–Bear Spread) Mixed
+8%
Weekly survey of individual investors: are you bullish, bearish, or neutral? The spread (bulls minus bears) above +30% = dangerous complacency. Below −20% = fear = contrarian buy signal. The crowd is reliably wrong at extremes.
🎓 Marks's iron rule: "When everyone is bullish, who's left to buy?" If 70% of investors are optimistic and already invested, there are few people left to drive prices higher. The next surprise tends to go against the consensus.
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
📊 AAII.com (free, weekly)
Watch for extremes: Bulls >60% or Bears >50%
BofA Fund Manager Cash Level Low
4.0%
Monthly BofA survey of 300+ professional fund managers: how much cash are they holding? Below 4% = everyone is fully invested = no dry powder = markets vulnerable. Above 6% = fear = contrarian buy signal. One of Marks's favourite tools.
🎓 If all your friends have already put their savings into a hot new restaurant, who's left to invest more? Nobody. When fund managers hold no cash, there's no new money to push prices higher — making markets fragile to any bad news.

CBOE Equity Put/Call Ratio Neutral
0.78
Ratio of put options (bets on decline) to call options (bets on rise). Below 0.7 = dangerous complacency — investors aren't buying protection. Above 1.2 = extreme fear = often near a market bottom. Use 10-day moving average to smooth noise.
🎓 Count how many people bought umbrella insurance vs. sunshine holiday insurance before summer. Almost nobody buying umbrellas (low put/call) means investors are overconfident it won't rain. Markets love to rain when everyone's unprepared.

Dalio's Three Cycle Framework

Before any KPI makes sense, you need to understand what economic machine you're reading. Here's the entire model in plain language.

01
The Short-Term Debt Cycle (5–8 years)
Driven entirely by credit expansion and contraction. Banks lend → people spend → economy grows → inflation builds → central bank raises rates → credit tightens → recession → rates fall → repeat. The Credit Impulse and Yield Curve tell you exactly where you are in this loop.
02
The Long-Term Debt Cycle (50–75 years)
Each short-term cycle ends with slightly more total debt than before. Over decades this accumulation becomes structural. Debt levels become so high that normal monetary policy stops working. We are in a late-stage long-term cycle. Options: deflation, inflationary devaluation, or restructuring. This is why gold and real assets deserve a structural allocation.
03
Productivity Growth (The Secular Trend)
Underneath all cycles, productivity is what actually drives long-term wealth creation. Technology, innovation, and institutional quality compound over time regardless of debt cycles. The danger: debt supercycles can suppress productivity for a decade or more — Japan post-1990 is the canonical warning.
04
Howard Marks: The Psychology Overlay
Marks adds the crucial psychological dimension. Markets cycle between greed and fear faster than the economic cycle below them. Even in a healthy economy, markets can be dangerously overvalued. His tools: sentiment surveys, positioning data, IPO quality, leverage levels. When everyone is optimistic and fully invested, future returns are low and risk is high — regardless of economic data.
"You can't predict. You can prepare. The goal isn't to know exactly where we are in the cycle — it's to know whether to lean toward aggression or defense, and by how much, based on the weight of evidence across all indicators simultaneously."
— Synthesis of Dalio + Marks + 100 years of market history
Dalio's "Beautiful" vs. "Ugly" Deleveraging
When a debt cycle peaks, societies must reduce their debt burden through four levers: austerity (deflationary, painful), debt restructuring (creditors take losses), wealth redistribution (taxes), and money printing (central bank monetises debt).

A "beautiful deleveraging" balances all four — enough money printing to avoid depression-level deflation, but not so much that it causes hyperinflation. The US 2010–2019 period was broadly this.

An "ugly deleveraging" uses too much of one lever (usually printing) — causing inflation alongside economic weakness: stagflation. In this scenario gold, commodities and real assets dramatically outperform while both stocks AND bonds fail simultaneously — exactly what 2022 previewed.
How to Read This Dashboard

A practical 5-step monthly protocol. Don't react to any single indicator — look for confluence across all four quadrants.

01
Check Credit Signals First
Every month: yield curve, HY spreads, credit impulse. These lead everything else by 6–12 months. If 2 of 3 flash warning, prepare to reduce risk.
02
Cross-Check with Labor
Every Thursday: initial jobless claims. Monthly: Sahm Rule and JOLTS quits. Labor confirms or denies credit signals. It can't be faked.
03
Read Sentiment Independently
Check BofA FM survey, AAII, and put/call ratio WITHOUT looking at prices first. Is the crowd fearful or greedy? Extreme readings are actionable.
04
Assess the Inflation Regime
Check 5yr/5yr expectations and real rates. Inflation rising + growth slowing = stagflation → gold and commodities. Falling + slowing = deflationary → long bonds and cash.
05
Require Confluence to Act
Only shift allocation when 5+ indicators agree. Move in thirds — never all at once. The goal is calibration over time, not binary market timing.

The Master Lesson
Ray Dalio Says
Credit creates cycles. Debt accumulates over generations. The machine is mechanical — but human emotion makes it irregular. Understand the template. Don't fight it. Diversify across assets that do well in different environments. The All Weather portfolio exists precisely because no one can know the future with certainty.
Howard Marks Says
You will never know exactly where we are. But you can know whether to lean toward aggression or defense. The biggest risk is not volatility — it's permanent loss of capital. That risk rises when optimism is extreme and valuations are stretched. Be a contrarian: fearful when others are greedy, greedy when others are fearful.
The Analyst Says
Watch the dashboard monthly. Require confluence of 5+ indicators before major shifts. Move in thirds — never all at once. In late cycle: reduce equity beta, raise quality, add gold and short-duration bonds, build cash. The patient investor who survives every cycle ends up owning the world. Patience is the ultimate edge.
